*September 2026 · ~50 targeted searches · supersedes the earlier findings file*

## 1. Executive summary

**Your instincts were right on all three pushbacks.**

1. **Likeness is the product, not a nice-to-have.** A 2025 RCT (315 kids, ages 5–6) found a story with the child's photo + name increased sharing significantly more than the same story without it; a 2021 study found *name-only* personalization did nothing. The free competitor (Google Gemini Storybook) has an obvious gap: characters drift page-to-page, likeness is "hit-or-miss," and there's no series. **Consistent likeness across an ongoing series is a defensible, paid-worthy feature.**
2. **$79/yr is too high to start.** RevenueCat's Education median is $43.94/yr. The closest AI-story comp (Oscar) is $49/yr. Epic/Moshi reach $79.99 on the back of huge libraries. Start at **~$49/yr, all kids included**, and push ARPU up with print and voice, not the base price.
3. **The podcast isn't the business.** No evidence it converts; drop it as the funnel. The first "wow" (your kid as the hero) *is* the funnel — make it free and shareable, especially to grandparents (they spend ~$2,654/yr on grandkids; AARP 2026).

**What the evidence changes about the product:**
- **Human-written stories + AI-drawn kids.** Parents accept AI illustrations far more than AI-written text (NC State 2025). Generation itself is commoditized (Gemini is free).
- **Avatar builder, not photo upload.** Child photos = COPPA personal info; AI-avatar apps face biometric suits; 72% of parents rate photo privacy important.
- **Story rules backed by research:** human hero, reward the virtue (don't punish the vice), model one concrete behavior, ask the child to *explain* the moral, pause-and-ask prompts, end on a cliffhanger.
- **Habit design:** 5 nights/week target, not 7; praise and story recognition, never prizes for being good.
- **The 6–9 gap is an opportunity:** read-aloud collapses after 6–8, yet 40% of 6–11-year-olds wish it continued.

## 4. Recommended product definition

**Positioning:** *"Your kids, drawn into a story that grows with them — one virtue a week, written by people, read by you."*

**Free (the "aha," under 2 minutes):**
- Avatar builder for each child (hair, skin, eyes, glasses, freckles, favorite outfit) → instant hero portrait
- Chapter 1 of the saga starring them, with 1–2 illustrations
- Weekly fridge card (printable) + curated classics library

**Family — $5.99/mo or $49/yr, all siblings included** (founding-family rate $39/yr, test $39/$49/$59):
- Every chapter nightly (human-written saga + classic retellings), each child drawn in, siblings co-star
- Missions, "spotted it" legends (parent approves every line), 5-of-7 lamps
- Printable booklets, chapter-book mode for ages 6–9

**Heirloom — ~$89/yr or add-ons** (raises ARPU without raising the entry price):
- Narration in your or a grandparent's voice (record or consented clone)
- One printed hardcover of the child's season per year (or $34–44 à la carte)
- Grandparent gift subscriptions and share links

**Unit-economics guardrails:** 1 personalized "hero moment" image per chapter + shared scene art (~$1.20/family/mo at $0.04/image); parent reads aloud by default; AI narration only on Heirloom.

**Top 5 to build first:**
1. **Avatar builder + consistent hero portrait** — biggest evidence-backed lever; beats the free competitor; no photo risk.
2. **Human-written Season 1 (8–12 chapters) with editorial rules** — human hero, reward-not-punish, one modeled behavior, explain-why prompt, cliffhanger.
3. **Free chapter 1 → paywall onboarding** — the child sees themselves within 60 seconds (Day-0 matters).
4. **Printable booklet + fridge card** — screen-free bedtime, print-learning edge, leads to the paid hardcover.
5. **Grandparent share link** — a story card showing the grandchild as the hero; cheapest distribution test, and it leads to gift subscriptions.

**Deprioritize:** podcast, photo upload, multilingual, native apps, AI-written stories as the default path.

## 6. Risks & cheap experiments

| Question | Experiment | Success signal | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does likeness drive the habit? | 10-family, 3-week pilot (incl. yours): avatar vs name-only, alternating weeks | ≥1 more night/week and more "read it again" requests in avatar weeks | ~$0 |
| What price? | Fake-door pricing page, $39 / $49 / $59, 3 variants | Click-to-checkout rate; pick the revenue-max point | ~$50 ads or community posts |
| Do grandparents buy? | Share link with the grandchild's hero portrait + "gift a year" | ≥5% of shares → gift purchases | ~$0 |
| Is AI art quality acceptable? | Show 20 parents avatar vs Gemini output; rate likeness and emotion match | ≥70% prefer ours; no "they look mad" mismatches | ~$0 |
| Will parents pay for print? | Pre-sell a Season 1 personalized hardcover | 30+ pre-orders at $39 | POD sample ~$50 |
| Does the saga sustain interest? | Track chapter completion and cliffhanger → next-night return in the pilot | ≥70% next-night return | ~$0 |
| Legal | COPPA/state-law review of the avatar-only design | Written sign-off | $2–5K |

## 7. Corrections to earlier claims
- "64%→41% read aloud" (attributed to Scholastic): **wrong source.** Common Sense 2025: daily reading among 5–8-year-olds fell from 64% to 52% since 2017. Scholastic: 55% of 0–5 read to 5+ days/week.
- "7+ hours of screens" (on the live site): wrong for ages 0–8; the actual figure is ~2.5 hrs/day (Common Sense 2025).
- Identity-label effect ("be a helper"): overstated; weak replication.
- "74% would pay a premium for personalization": unverifiable.
- The "story remembers" mechanic isn't unique (Lulawe).
