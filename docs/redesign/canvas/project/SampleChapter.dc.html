The Last Light of Candlemere · Chapter 1 · Brave

The Fox at the Window

The lights of Candlemere were going out, one by one, and nobody knew why. Tonight Clara watched from the window as the lamp over the Moot Hall door shrank to the size of a seed, and went out. Now the only light left in the whole village was Grandma Ruth's fire, crackling behind her. Then something tapped on the glass. Tap. Tap. Tap.

Clara pulled back the curtain. On the sill sat a red fox, wet to the ears, with a leaf stuck to one of them. "Good evening," said the fox. "I'm so sorry to bother you."

Hugh came to stand beside her. Alfie pushed his glasses up his nose. "Foxes don't talk," he said. "This one does," said the fox. "And she is very cold."

Clara looked back. "Grandma, she's cold!" Grandma was already smiling at the fox, and she nodded. "Come in, fox," said Clara. Hugh lifted the latch, and in she came, and shook herself dry all over the hearth-rug, and all over Alfie.

"Clara let a cold, wet fox into the house. Why do you think she did?"

Grandma looked at the fox for a long moment, the way you look at an old friend. "Well," she said. "You've got your grandmother's tail." "Everyone says that," said the fox. "My name is Ember. And the beacon on Wardlow has gone out."

Clara looked at Hugh. Hugh looked at Alfie. And that is how it began.
