The Lantern in the Attic

The attic stairs creaked the way they only creak at night. Theo went up first, because he was eight and because somebody had to. June came next, holding a paintbrush like a sword. Max came last, holding June.

In the corner, under a quilt that smelled like Grandpa's coat, something glowed. It was a lantern of old brass, and tied to its handle was a note: For the ones brave enough to carry it.

"Is it hot?" whispered June. Theo reached out. His hand shook. So he took one slow breath, in through his nose and out like blowing out a birthday candle, and wrapped his fingers around the handle.

It wasn't hot. It was warm, like a mug of cocoa. The attic window swung open by itself, and where the backyard should have been, a road of silver stones ran up into the hills.

Pause & ask
"What did Theo do when his hand shook? Could you try that?"

At the bottom of the road sat a small red fox with one sore paw. "Finally," said the fox. "I'm Pip. The lights of Brindlewick are going out, one by one, and only a lantern-bearer can bring them back."

Theo looked at June. June looked at Max. Max looked at the fox and said, "We'll help." And that is how it began.
