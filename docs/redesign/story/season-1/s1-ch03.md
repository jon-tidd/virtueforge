## Chapter-book telling

Last time: at every door on the lane the answer was "Not I," until Hugh said, "I'll help."

Today the flame would leave Candlemere. After lunch, Grandma took down the old brass lantern. "The Keepers' Lantern," she said.

Grandma opened the little door, which only grown-ups ever open, and held the lantern close to the hearth. "Fire won't be taken," she said. "It has to be given, and somebody has to ask."

"Please," whispered Clara.

Everyone waited in a hush, the way you wait for a sneeze…and the flame hopped across. The whole lantern glowed like a jar of honey.

"I wasn't worried at all," said Ember, from behind the log basket. Her ears went flat. *Atchoo!*

"Now, the lantern breath," said Grandma. "In slowly through your nose. Out so gently that a flame would only lean."

Everyone tried it, even Ember, who was always willing. Nothing happened at all, and she looked terribly pleased.

Then Grandma turned the lantern over. Underneath were names, scratched into the brass. "That one's mine," she said. "I carried this lantern to Wardlow when I was seven."

Hugh read the names, sounding out the hard ones. One was worn so smooth that nobody could read it.

"Whose is that one, worn smooth?" asked Alfie.

Grandma ran a thumb over the smooth place. "That's not mine to tell."

Ember looked at the smooth name for a long moment, and said nothing at all.

"By the old law," said Grandma, "any child who carries this lantern is fed and housed at every door on the Keepers' Way." Then Grandma cast on a red scarf. "One row for every night you're away."

At the gate there were hugs all round, and a proper goodbye. Then off they went in the gold of the afternoon, Hugh carrying the lantern as steady as steady.

All afternoon the Keepers' Way ran west between tall hedges, past stubble fields and a mill-pond and a line of old grey milestones. Ember trotted in front with her tail up like a flag, and stepped round every single puddle. "Wet paws," she said, "are the enemy of a good fox."

The hedges were full of rosehips, as red as Ember's coat. A robin followed them for a whole field, as if it wanted to see where the lantern was going.

Once, a brown hare peeked out of the hedge, stared at the lantern, and lolloped off as if it had just remembered a kettle on the boil.

Ember bowed to it most politely, but it did not bow back. "No manners at all," she sniffed.

Hugh read every milestone out loud, sounding out the old carved letters: how far to the next village, and how far back home. Home was already quite a long way.

Clara hummed a hearth song from home to every cow they passed, and every cow looked up to listen. Alfie counted the cows.

"One, two, three, four, lots," said Ember, helping.

And in the bottom of the backpack, under the apples, rode one small treasure from home: a smooth grey pebble from the cottage path, for luck.

By the time the sun was low and orange, Candlemere was only a smudge of roofs behind them. Nobody talked about it, but everyone looked back once.

At sunset the road passed a hollow oak as wide as a doorway. Far ahead stood a shepherd's hut. Then the wind began to rise.

*Whoo*, went the wind. Inside the lantern the flame began to flicker, and shrank and shrank, until it was only a tiny blue bead.

Hugh's heart thumped. Part of him wanted to dash on into the dark. And part of him wanted to run all the way home.

Hild's pocket question was still there, like a smooth stone. Hugh asked it out loud: "What's the brave thing, and what's the silly thing?"

[[Pause & ask]]

The wind only blew harder. "Excuse me," said Ember to the wind, very grandly. "That is my tail, and I should like it back."

"Ember, what should we do?" asked Hugh.

"I know the way home," said Ember gently. "The way on is yours."

"The silly thing is running on in the dark," said Hugh slowly. "The brave thing is stopping here, and keeping the flame out of the wind."

Hugh took a lantern breath, the way Grandma had shown them, and looked around.

"Big stone!" said Clara, pointing.

It was an old grey milestone, with a little lantern carved on top. Hugh read the carving: "West to Wardlow. East to home." The wind roared past one side. On the other, it was almost still.

"The lee," said Hugh. That was Grandma's word for the side of a wall the wind can't reach.

Ember sat down on the still side. "Much better," she said. "My tail agrees."

Alfie looked at the dark, and his tummy went wobbly. Then Alfie held an invisible lantern up high, took one step toward the milestone, and said, "I'll help. Lantern up!"

Clara gave a yawn as big as a barn door, so Ember wrapped that magnificent red tail round her like a scarf. "Night night, little flame," whispered Clara.

Overhead the stars came out, until the sky was sprinkled like sugar on a dark loaf. Even Ember didn't try to count them.

Inside the lantern the blue bead flickered and bowed, like someone very tired, but it stayed steady.

Far behind them, Grandma would be knitting the first row of the red scarf, by a fire that stayed awake. Hugh held on to that, the way you hold a warm cup in cold hands.

Hugh wanted to cup the little flame in both hands. But hands go on your knees, never on the lantern: that was Grandma's rule.

Hugh set the lantern down in the lee of the milestone, and he and Alfie made a wall with their backs to the wind. Clara was already asleep in Ember's tail.

Ember lifted her nose. "Someone else walked this road tonight," she said. "Someone I know."

"Who?" whispered Alfie.

"Sleep now." The flame was only a blue bead. Would it hold till morning?

## Picture-book telling

All afternoon the road ran west, and Ember stepped round every puddle. Soon Candlemere was only a smudge of roofs. Nobody said so, but everyone looked back once.

At sunset everyone passed a hollow oak, as wide as a doorway. Far ahead stood a shepherd's hut. Then *whoo*, the wind rose, and the flame shrank to a tiny blue bead.

Hugh's heart thumped in the dark. Then Hugh stopped, and asked the pocket question out loud. "What's the brave thing, and what's the silly thing?"

Ember wouldn't choose the way on, so Hugh took a lantern breath. "Big stone!" said Clara, pointing. It was an old milestone, with one side still, out of the wind.

"I'll help," said Alfie, holding an invisible lantern up high. "Lantern up!" The blue flame flickered and bowed, like someone very tired, but it stayed steady.

"The silly thing is running on in the dark," said Hugh slowly. "The brave thing is stopping here, and keeping the flame out of the wind." Hugh set the lantern down in the lee. Everyone made a wall, hands on knees.

Clara fell asleep in Ember's tail. "Someone I know walked this road tonight," said Ember. The flame was only a blue bead. Would it hold till morning?
